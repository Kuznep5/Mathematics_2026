### Exercise 1. Definition of the Inverse
 
#### Solution
 
By definition, $B=A^{-1}$ if and only if $AB=BA=I$. First product:
 
$$
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
2\cdot1+1\cdot(-1) & 2\cdot(-1)+1\cdot2 \\
1\cdot1+1\cdot(-1) & 1\cdot(-1)+1\cdot2 \\
\end{pmatrix}
$$
 
Hence
 
$$
AB=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 \\
\end{pmatrix}
=I.
$$
 
Second product:
 
$$
BA=
\begin{pmatrix}
1\cdot2+(-1)\cdot1 & 1\cdot1+(-1)\cdot1 \\
(-1)\cdot2+2\cdot1 & (-1)\cdot1+2\cdot1 \\
\end{pmatrix}
$$
 
Hence
 
$$
BA=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
0 & 1 \\
\end{pmatrix}
=I.
$$
 
Both products equal $I$, so $B=A^{-1}$.
